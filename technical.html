Why Surface-Only Temperature Fails Disaster Forecasting

Satellite SST was nearly flat across the central Bay of Bengal. But OceanEmbed-PG reveals that the 26°C isotherm plunged past 100 metres depth, insulated by fresh river runoff and a barrier layer. The storm never experienced cold water negative feedback, allowing unchecked intensification.

Two Oceans, Two Distinct Physics Regimes

Bay of Bengal (BoB)

Salinity-Stratified
  • Ganga-Brahmaputra runoff forms pronounced barrier layers (BLT).
  • Warm subsurface water is insulated from atmospheric wind-mixing.
  • SSS is the dominant predictor; SST alone causes severe underprediction.
  • Exhibits winter temperature inversions — must not constrain monotonic decrease.
  • Thermocline RMSE: ~0.88°C at 100m.

Arabian Sea (AS)

Wind-Driven
  • High-evaporation, saline basin dominated by vigorous southwest monsoon winds.
  • Deep mechanical mixing with classical thermocline structures.
  • SLA is the dominant predictor for adiabatic thermocline heaving.
  • Intense Ekman coastal upwelling along the western boundary.
  • Thermocline RMSE: ~0.77°C at 100m.

The Halocline Exception

Standard naive models force monotonic temperature decrease with depth. This fails in the Bay of Bengal, where massive freshwater runoff caps warmer subsurface waters during winter. Our conditional stability term preserves these real inversion phenomena.
